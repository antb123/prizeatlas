Science Prizes Rarely Stay Home, Unless They Are American or German

Of 16 leading science prizes, only four have given more than two in five of their awards to scientists working in the host country: three American prizes and Germany’s Max Planck Medal. Norway’s Abel and Kavli prizes have never gone to a scientist based in Norway.

PrizeAtlas looked at where every winner of 16 leading science prizes was working when they won, and set that against the country that hosts each prize.

The three American prizes keep most of their awards at home: the Turing Award 77%, the Lasker Award 75% and the Breakthrough Prize 67%. That says as much about American science as about home bias. The US is the leading base for 14 of the 16 prizes, and 48% of Nobel laureates in physics, chemistry and medicine were working there when they won.

The Max Planck Medal is the one prize outside the US whose host country leads its own table. Forty of its 90 medalists since 1929 were based in Germany, against 18 in the US.

Japan’s two prizes come next. The Japan Prize has gone to scientists based in Japan 20% of the time and the Kyoto Prize 17%, and in both Japan is second only to the US.

Scandinavia sits at the other end. Only 17 of 668 Nobel science laureates were working in Sweden when they won, and Sweden’s Crafoord Prize has gone home once in 82 awards. Norway’s Abel Prize and Kavli Prize have never gone to a scientist based in Norway, and Denmark’s Brain Prize has twice. The Brain Prize is also the only one of the 16 led by the UK, with 16 winners to the US’s 15.

Israel and Canada sit in between. The Wolf Prize has gone to scientists based in Israel 8% of the time, second only to the US, and the Canada Gairdner International Award to scientists in Canada 13%.

Notes

A winner’s base is the country of the institution they were affiliated with when the award was made. One winner with posts in two countries counts in both, and a scientist who won the same prize twice counts twice. The Shaw Prize is awarded in Hong Kong and counted as China; its five home winners were all based on the mainland. Two Breakthrough Prize winners have no recorded affiliation and count only in the total. The Fields Medal is awarded by the International Mathematical Union and has no host country. Countries use today’s borders. The data are in the PrizeAtlas explorer and the full dataset.
