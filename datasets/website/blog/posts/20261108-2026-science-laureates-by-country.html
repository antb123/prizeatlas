US Runs Away With the 2026 Science Prize Table as Japan and Germany Tie for Second

The US leads the 2026 science prize standings by a wide margin, with 29 of the 63 winners named so far, while Japan and Germany are level in second place on eight each.

It works like an Olympic medal table. PrizeAtlas tallies the winners of 14 leading prizes, from the Nobel and the Fields Medal to the Kavli and Wolf prizes, by country, and keeps two tables: one by where winners were working when they won, the other by where they were born. The standings run through Nov. 8, and more 2026 results are still to come, including the Turing Award, which isn’t announced until next year.

On the main table, by where winners work, the US is far ahead. Japan and Germany share second, France is fourth with seven and the UK fifth with four. Israel takes sixth with two, and six countries, China among them, have one each.

The birthplace table is tighter at the top. The US still leads, but with 13, ahead of Japan on eight. Germany and France tie for third on seven, China moves up to fifth with five, and Russia finishes sixth with four despite not a single winner on home soil.

It is also a much bigger field. Winners were born in 22 countries but work in only 12, which leaves 11 countries, among them India, Italy, Spain and Argentina, on the birthplace table but off the main one.

Setting one table against the other shows who gains and who loses. The US is plus 16, the biggest swing in either direction, with 17 of its 29 winners born elsewhere. China and Russia are minus four each.

Japan’s eight are all home-grown and all at home, the only large total with no movement either way. Israel makes the main table without a single native-born winner: both of its entries, the mathematicians Joseph Bernstein and David Kazhdan, who shared the Wolf Prize, were born in Moscow.

France finishes level on seven in both tables, though with a different lineup. Two French-born winners, mathematician Emmanuel Candès and sleep researcher Emmanuel Mignot, are at Stanford University, while Fields medalist Hong Wang and British-born astronomer Rodrigo Ibata won for French institutions.

The tables will be updated as the remaining 2026 winners are named.
